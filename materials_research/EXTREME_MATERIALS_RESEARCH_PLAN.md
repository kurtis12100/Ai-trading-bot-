# Advanced materials discovery — research plan (2026-11-20)

**Status: research design, not validated discoveries.** Keep this work separate from the lithium-metal halide battery candidate screen. Avoid publishing specific potentially patentable novel formulations in this public repository.

## Track A — high-temperature / ablative thermal protection (first priority)
Reference families: ultra-high-temperature ceramics (ZrB2, HfB2, ZrC, HfC), SiC-containing composites, carbon-phenolic ablators and ceramic matrix composites. These are established reference systems, **not new discoveries**.

Metrics: oxidation resistance in air, decomposition/phase stability, heat flux and exposure time, recession rate (mm/s), mass loss, backside temperature, thermal conductivity, density, thermal shock resistance, manufacturability. Distinguish reusable insulation from sacrificial ablation. In air, melting point alone is not enough.

## Track B — passive blast/impact protection
Reference concepts: ceramic front face with ductile backing; metallic or polymeric cellular cores; fiber-reinforced laminates. Assess specific energy absorption (J/kg), fragment containment, areal density (kg/m2), repeatability and failure mode. Work on civilian protective applications and passive material properties, not explosive design.

## Track C — strength-to-weight
Reference concepts: precipitation-strengthened aluminum, titanium alloys, high-entropy alloys, fiber-reinforced composites. Measure specific yield strength (MPa/(g/cm3)), ductility, fracture toughness, fatigue and corrosion. '100x stronger than steel' is not a defensible goal without specifying properties and conditions.

## Stage gates
1. Build baseline list of known compounds/architectures and reliable property measurements with citations.
2. Enumerate variations within realistic composition/processing constraints, retaining source and generation history.
3. Check novelty in scientific literature, crystallographic databases and patents; mark unverified rather than 'new'.
4. Screen thermodynamic stability and oxidation mechanisms using appropriate phase diagrams and first-principles tools, with uncertainty.
5. Rank on application-specific metrics, cost, manufacturability and safety. Commission lab tests before claiming performance.

## Immediate research shortlist (known baselines only)
- ZrB2–SiC composite: oxidation-resistant UHTC research baseline; silicon-rich oxide can protect in certain regimes.
- HfB2–SiC composite: high-temperature reference; heavy and expensive.
- Carbon–phenolic: established sacrificial ablator; requires actual ablation tests.
- SiC/SiC ceramic matrix composite: reusable high-temperature structural reference; oxidation/environment limits.
- Ceramic-face/ductile-back composite: passive impact protection reference; architecture not a novel compound.

**No computational thermodynamic simulations, literature-wide novelty search, or physical tests have been completed by this document.**
