# First experimental evidence gate — steel non-wetting coatings (20 November 2026)

## Verified published comparator
A published study tested molten steel on an alumina substrate, and on alumina with an alumina–carbon coating, at 1520 °C. At time zero, angles were 129.2 ± 1.5° (uncoated) and 137.7 ± 1.5° (30 wt% carbon coated). **Difference: +8.5°**, with independently reported ±1.5° values. This supports reduced initial wetting in that specific experiment, NOT 50% more furnace cycles, improved metal drainage, better slag resistance, or a new invention. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0955221917308105

Another 2025 peer-reviewed study compared molten iron on zirconia, alumina, magnesia and other oxides at 1823 K using a sessile-drop method. Cross-study results cannot be interpreted as a directly controlled coating comparison. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11913070/

2026 dynamic slag-corrosion research at 1600–1660 °C found certain no-cement in-situ spinel-forming castables more resistant than tested low-cement castables. The comparison concerns corrosion, not non-wetting or lining lifetime, and must not be converted into an assumed 50% gain. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0955221926002967

**Important counter-evidence:** zirconia may be non-wetting to liquid iron but can be wetted/corroded by metallurgical slags. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0955221911006418

## What is and is not proven
- Proven *in cited published experiment*: an alumina–carbon surface yielded +8.5° initial contact angle vs alumina under stated conditions.
- Not proven: relative ease of release after cooling, recession rate, contamination safety, repeated-cycle durability, suitability to an industrial crucible, novelty, profitability, or +50% service life.
- 50% longer life is a **target**, not a result.

## Commercial experiment specification
Choose a **specific iron or steel foundry melting operation** and characterize substrate, molten alloy, slag basicity, working temperature, dwell time, furnace atmosphere, current lifetime and failure mode.

Perform blind-coded controlled tests (>=3 independent replicates per group, with full confidence intervals when feasible):
A. Incumbent commercial lining (uncoated)
B. Commercial alumina–spinel
C. Zirconia-rich reference
D. Alumina–carbon reference ONLY if alloy carbon-pickup specifications and oxygen conditions permit.

**Two independent test gates:** (1) molten-steel contact angle and retained-metal mass after a matched draining/cooling procedure, plus metal contamination (C, O and inclusions); (2) refractory recession/penetration and spallation under the actual slag chemistry and thermal cycling. Prefer matched tests in a qualified high-temperature laboratory. Do not extrapolate contact angles alone to industrial service life.

**Stop conditions:** severe slag reaction, coating flaking, unsafe cracks or metal contamination. Measure installed cost per melt and actual +50% life only after an industrial validation program.

## Evidence quality and novelty
The alumina–carbon demonstration is established prior art, not a candidate proprietary recipe. Verify 2018 paper publication metadata, the precise experimental composition and conditions before replicating. Do not publish unfiled proprietary formulations to public GitHub. No internal physical tests or materials simulation have been completed.
