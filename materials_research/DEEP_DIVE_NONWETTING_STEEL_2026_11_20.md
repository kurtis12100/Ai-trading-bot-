# Deep dive: non-wetting refractories for steel / iron (2026-11-20)

**Objective:** screen protective lining approaches that reduce molten-metal/slag sticking, corrosion and replacement cost. **This document is prior-art assessment, not a novel invention or a tested product.** Repo is public; do not disclose any unfiled proprietary recipes.

## Critical finding: contact angle depends on conditions
A 2025 direct sessile-drop comparison of liquid Fe on oxide substrates at 1823 K (~1550 C) found late-stage angles of ZrO2 143°, Al2O3 136°, MgO 122°, SiO2 116° and CaO 104°. Higher angles indicate less wetting *in that test*; they do NOT establish overall lining life or resistance to slag. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11913070/

Other studies report varying values, including ~137° for MgO and ~98° for Al2O3 under different experiments. Oxygen pressure, steel chemistry, hold time, substrate texture and reaction layers alter outcomes. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/srin.202200086 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2238785422017513

## Candidate research pathways (known materials, not new discoveries)
1. **Al2O3-MgO / MgAl2O4 spinel working lining**, strongest practical baseline for steel slag corrosion. 2026 study of dynamic corrosion at 1600–1660 C found a no-cement in-situ spinel-forming composition with 5 wt% MgO outperformed compared low-cement, preformed-spinel castables under the study conditions; microsilica removal improved resistance. This specific variation is already published and NOT patentable as new just by repeating it. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0955221926002967
2. **ZrO2-rich non-wetting surface**, a promising *metal-release* benchmark given liquid Fe sessile-drop results, but expensive, prone to phase/thermal-expansion issues depending on stabilization, and requires evaluation against real steel slag. No claim of better furnace performance.
3. **Al2O3-based oxide/carbon or spinel-carbon protective surfaces**, possible metal non-wetting and slag control but oxidation and carbon pickup are major use-specific constraints. 2025 study saw initial contact angles of 121° vs 129° for specific alumina-spinel-carbon configurations; not inherently superior. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S095522192500809X
4. **MgO-C** for highly basic slag lines: incumbent baseline with carbon oxidation/corrosion tradeoffs, not a new formulation. https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adem.202502888

## Practical test design
For **non-pressure-bearing, replaceable foundry coupons** rather than a production furnace, choose one steel grade, slag composition, working temperature (~1550–1650 C for many steel applications, alloy dependent), and exposure duration in consultation with a qualified lab.

Compare baseline commercial alumina-spinel, zirconia-rich reference, and incumbent commercial MgO/MgO-C where applicable. Use >=3 independent replicates per test group. A qualified laboratory should run (a) molten-metal sessile drop at controlled oxygen activity (angle at t0 and at 5/30/60 min where feasible), (b) dynamic slag-corrosion or rotating finger test, (c) repeated thermal-shock cycles, (d) microstructural SEM/EDS and XRD, and (e) melt cleanliness / inclusion pickup measurement.

Track **wetting angle**, *retained metal mass* after standardized draining/cooling, slag penetration depth, wall recession per melt, crack/spall area, cycles to failure, coating contamination and total installed cost per heat. Report standard deviations and failure modes. A high contact angle alone does not prove release performance.

## Decision rules
Reject anything that shows dangerous slag penetration, delamination, metal contamination, accelerated substrate erosion, or unsafe thermal cracking. Prioritize demonstrably improved cost-per-heat or life under controlled conditions. Do not propose home melt trials or use a coating as a structural/safety-critical liner without professional qualification.

## Commercial positioning
Potential first market: reusable foundry tools, ladle accessories and crucible release surfaces with defined alloys, not a universal coating for blast furnace hearths. Customer discovery with an existing foundry and supplier is essential. No patentability, cost saving or revenue has been established.
