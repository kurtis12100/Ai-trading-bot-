# Evidence-based opportunity scan — 2026-11-20

**Status:** literature-backed research directions only. No novel composition, patentability, lab performance, or investment return established. Do not store proprietary candidate recipes in this public repo.

## Research-backed findings

- A 2026 review reports that conventional ultra-high-temperature ceramics still face low fracture toughness, thermal-shock sensitivity, oxidation and densification problems. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925838826047559
- NASA's research on additive-manufactured thermal protection emphasizes continuous heat shields, graded surfaces and reduced seams/installation burden; these are existing ideas, not newly invented here. https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/117408
- NASA notes temperature-dependent thermochemical instabilities in Si-containing high-temperature ceramics, including changes in oxidation mechanism, porosity, conductivity and surface properties. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240004489
- A 2026 ZrB2-SiC-ZrC experimental study reports ablation resistance under one 2000 °C / 40 s oxy-acetylene test, but strength falls at higher temperature in the tested conditions. Do **not** generalize the result to flight qualification. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272884226030713
- Experimental comparisons of oxidation resistance across high-entropy carbides and borides exist, demonstrating that this is an active prior-art area, not unexplored territory. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666539524000270

## Opportunity ranking (hypotheses, not discoveries)

1. **Low-cost oxidation-resistant coating for industrial hot parts.** Target measurable improvement over standard ZrB2-SiC or SiC coatings after repeated thermal cycles. Lower-cost industrial furnaces or processing equipment might be a more accessible early customer than flight hardware.
2. **Functionally graded protective coating / repair process.** Address delamination and thermal expansion mismatch between substrate and coating. NASA already explores graded TPS, so the idea is prior art; any novelty must lie in a specific demonstrably new chemistry, process or result.
3. **Toughened ultra-high-temperature ceramic composite.** Aim to preserve oxidation performance while improving crack resistance and cyclic thermal shock. Competitive and expensive to validate.

## Experiments needed before commercialization claims

- Select baseline substrate and coating; identify what temperature, atmosphere and number of thermal cycles matter for target industry.
- Find independent data on phase stability, oxygen permeability, thermal expansion, thermal conductivity, fracture toughness and manufacturing cost.
- Design controlled coupons and reference specimens; measure mass change, oxide scale, cracks, adhesion and repeated-cycle failure.
- Perform prior-art searches and consult qualified patent counsel before disclosing specific recipes; CIPO recommends confidentiality before filing. https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/patents-learn-basics-inventing-next-big-thing-learn-why-patents-matter

## Commercial gate

Business value is **unproven**. Seek a specific customer pain point and achievable manufacturing/qualification route. A successful industrial coating may be easier to sell than a rocket heat shield requiring extensive certification. That is a commercialization hypothesis, not market validation.
