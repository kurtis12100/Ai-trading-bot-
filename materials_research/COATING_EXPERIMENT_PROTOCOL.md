# Coating opportunity: comparative screening protocol (20 Nov 2026)

**Commercial hypothesis:** lower-cost repairable oxidation-resistant coating for non-pressure-bearing furnace hardware. Not intended for safety-critical use without engineering approval. This is a research protocol; **no tests have been run**.

## Evidence / prior art
- Slurry-sprayed glass-ceramic coating on carbon-steel boiler components withstood at least 75 thermal-shock cycles between 800°C and room temperature without visible spallation/cracking in a published study: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272884224034205. This is prior art, not an original discovery.
- Slurry aluminide coatings on boiler steels face thermal-expansion mismatch and coating/substrate diffusion issues: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010938X20302754.
- 2026 review on utility-boiler corrosion coatings, including FeCrAl/alumina-forming coatings and Al-Si slurry approaches: https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/16/7/790.
- 2026 review of oxide-forming coatings and interface stability: https://www.mdpi.com/2504-477x/10/1/51.
- CIPO warns against public invention disclosure before filing: https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/learn-patents-canadian-intellectual-property-office.

## Controlled first experiment (lab proposal, not instructions for improvised testing)
**Use case:** replaceable non-pressure-bearing furnace racks/fixtures; choose metal substrate and working environment with a materials lab and supplier.

**Controls:** (A) uncoated substrate, (B) commercially sourced aluminide coating, (C) commercially sourced slurry glass-ceramic coating. Candidate experimental coating D is reserved pending a documented literature/patent search and supplier review. No new specific recipe is established here.

**Protocol design:** minimum three independent coupons per condition, matching material geometry, surface prep and coating thickness as closely as practical; record substrate grade, deposition and cure process, starting mass/area, measured layer thickness and photo micrographs. Laboratory furnace thermal cycling at a site-appropriate and approved temperature protocol (start with published 800°C benchmark **only if suitable for selected steel/substrate**); control atmosphere and exposure time. Use standardized adhesion and microscopy evaluations. Any pressure-part applicability needs separate engineering approval.

**Primary endpoints:** fractional coating spallation area, oxide-scale mass change per surface area (mg/cm²), coating adhesion, through-thickness cracking and substrate mass loss. **Secondary:** coating material/application cost per m², thermal-cycle count until first measurable damage and repairability.

**Initial candidate success gates (hypotheses, not guarantees):** 25% fewer cracks/spallation than the best relevant commercial control at an equal cycling protocol, or equal protection for >=25% less total installed cost; independently verify outcomes, document uncertainties and replication. Published 75-cycle result is a comparison reference, not a universally interchangeable test condition.
