Start here: what GPS tells you

Machine control compares the measured position of a configured machine reference point with a loaded digital design. It can show cut, fill, slope and guidance lines. The number on the display is only useful when the correct design, localization, calibration and site checks are verified.

Training simulation, not machine operating instructions. Confirm your exact equipment model and software version, follow manufacturer documentation and site procedures, and independently verify grade. GPS never replaces utility locating, exclusion zones or safe excavation practices.

What to check on a real machine

Confirm the project and design name; machine and attachment configuration; positioning quality and correction status; reference point; units; offsets; design surface; and a verified site control check. Never assume an icon or color has the same meaning across brands or versions.

Field lessons

1. Pre-start verification

Confirm approved plans, current design revision, machine setup, positioning quality and independent site control check before relying on guidance.

2. Cut versus fill

Compare measured elevation to target at the same reference point. Understand sign conventions and account for attachment geometry.

3. Excavator depth

Understand bucket-tip tracking, tilt and rotation sensor calibration, attachments and physical checks against the design.

4. Slopes and batters

Interpret design lines, cross sections and slope guidance. Never substitute a screen for trench stability or excavation protection requirements.

5. Design mismatch

If physical checks disagree with machine control, stop work, secure the area and resolve calibration, control, design and offset discrepancies.

6. Utility work

Verify locates and permits, follow required exposure methods, and maintain site-specific clearances. GPS models are not proof of buried utility location.
